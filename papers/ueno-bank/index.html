From Bank Failure to Political Crisis: The Economics of Ueno Bank’s Collapse in Paraguay

David M. Forsyth
Notes opened November 11, 2026. This is not a finished paper, and there is no PDF yet.

Question

Did public-sector deposits concentrate at Ueno Bank, relative to other banks in Paraguay, before the Central Bank’s board opened Ueno’s resolution on November 2, 2026?

A difference between Ueno and the rest of the banking system would document where public money sat. It would not, by itself, show that any official directed those deposits.

The figure

Ueno Bank: Financial Growth and Key Events. Each point is a year-end, in trillions of guaraníes. The vertical lines are the dates that already have a record. The heavier line is the Visión merger, and the shaded years are the first published year-ends that include the acquired bank.

Assets are total assets. Deposits are private-sector deposits plus public-sector deposits, and they leave out deposits from other financial institutions, accrued interest, and subordinated debt. Loans are performing loans to the non-financial sector, before provisions and accrued interest. In 2021 and 2022 the statement reported that loan stock as one line. From 2023 the private and public lines are added together. There is no 2026 year-end. The bank’s resolution began in November.

The 2021 and 2022 figures are Financiera Ueno, the same legal entity. The audited notes say banking operations began on December 1, 2023. On June 28, 2024, the same notes say the absorption of Visión Banco was completed and Visión’s assets, Gs. 7.623 trillion on that date, were brought into Ueno’s statements. The 2024 and 2025 points are the published totals after that incorporation. They are not a measure of growth by the bank that existed before the merger.

Dates tied to a record

  • August 15, 2023. Santiago Peña took the oath of office. The government news agency and the presidency both date the oath to that day.
  • Carlos Carvallo’s appointment still has three dates. The Senate agreed on August 30, 2023. Decree No. 214/2023 is reported on September 1, and the judiciary records the oath on September 7. The marker waits on the decree.
  • November 22, 2023. The 2024 audited notes record Central Bank Acta No. 67 of that date as the approval of the conversion and the banking license. The same notes say banking operations began on December 1, 2023. The board’s own resolution is not yet filed separately.
  • June 28, 2024. The 2024 audited notes say the absorption of Visión Banco was completed that day, and that Visión’s assets and liabilities were brought into Ueno’s statements from then on. Earlier steps remain part of the record: Resolution No. 32, Acta No. 23, of May 27, let the banks proceed, and Visión’s shareholders homologated the definitive agreement on June 17. The marker is the June 28 incorporation, not those earlier steps.
  • March 22, 2025. Ueno Holding’s extraordinary meeting approved the redemption and cancellation of Santiago Peña’s ordinary shares, among others. The company notified the market on April 3. The notice says the cancellation followed the remaining formalities.
  • November 2, 2026. The Central Bank’s board adopted Resolution No. 1, Acta No. 49, opening the resolution. The securities regulator cited that act the next day, when it suspended trading in Ueno’s securities. The public notice came in the early hours of November 3. The board’s own text is not yet in the file.
  • November 6, 2026. The Senate, sitting for the impeachment, declared Peña guilty of poor performance in office and removed him. The same day, a joint session of Congress swore in Pedro Alliana to finish the 2023–2028 term. The Senate’s account says the vote was nominal and unanimous among the senators present. The accusation is cited as Chamber of Deputies Resolution No. 5896/2026, and that text is not yet in the file. This was a congressional judgment. It was not a criminal conviction. Prosecutors have brought charges. Peña denies wrongdoing. This page does not treat the charges as established facts.

What would be measured

The comparison is bank by month, from 2019 through the resolution: public-sector deposits, total deposits, and capital ratios at Ueno and at the other Paraguayan banks. The source for that comparison is the Central Bank’s own statistics and its resolution record, not a running total from news reports.

Three pieces belong in the eventual paper, and only one of them is a measurement.

  • How the bank’s capital looked in the statements it published, and how it looked after any later reclassification.
  • Whether public deposits at Ueno diverged from public deposits at other banks.
  • How those losses reached households and the public purse, and then the presidency.

What is not posted yet

The figure above is the bank’s own published year-end totals. It does not compare Ueno with other banks, and it does not show the path inside a year. The regulatory-capital ratio is not on it. Law No. 5787/2016 sets a floor of 12 percent for total regulatory capital and also allows a higher requirement for a particular bank, so that line waits on the requirement that applied to Ueno. Reported figures for related-party loans and the number of depositors stay off this page until they come from a primary source. News accounts do not yet agree on the depositor count, and this page will not adopt one.
