From Bank Failure to Political Crisis: The Economics of Ueno Bank’s Collapse in Paraguay

David M. Forsyth
Notes opened November 11, 2026. This is not a finished paper, and there is no PDF yet.

Question

Did public-sector deposits concentrate at Ueno Bank, relative to other banks in Paraguay, before the Central Bank of Paraguay put Ueno into resolution on November 3, 2026?

A difference between Ueno and the rest of the banking system would document where public money sat. It would not, by itself, show that any official directed those deposits.

Why the timing matters

The resolution came on November 3. Congress removed President Santiago Peña on November 6, and Vice President Pedro Alliana took office the same day. Peña had held shares in Ueno’s parent company until March 2025. Prosecutors have brought charges connected to the collapse. He denies wrongdoing. Those proceedings are separate from this project, and this page does not treat the charges as established facts.

The failure reached so many households that the political crisis and the bank crisis are hard to separate. News accounts do not yet agree on the depositor count. Until the Central Bank publishes one figure, this page will not adopt one.

What would be measured

The comparison is bank by month, from 2019 through the resolution: public-sector deposits, total deposits, and capital ratios at Ueno and at the other Paraguayan banks. The source for that comparison is the Central Bank’s own statistics and its resolution record, not a running total from news reports.

Three pieces belong in the eventual paper, and only one of them is a measurement.

  • How the bank’s capital looked before related credits were reclassified, and how it looked after.
  • Whether public deposits at Ueno diverged from public deposits at other banks.
  • How those losses reached households and the public purse, and then the presidency.

What is not posted yet

There is no estimate here, and no chart. Reported figures for related-party loans, public deposits, and the number of depositors stay off this page until they come from a primary source.
